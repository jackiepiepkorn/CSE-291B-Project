Approach 2: Individual Peptide Row Analysis — Results Report


Introduction

In Approach 2, we treat each peptide row as a single biological unit and ask
whether that peptide shows a significant dose-response relationship with any
of the 46 kinase inhibitors in the experiment. Rather than focusing on one
drug at a time, this analysis considers the full response profile of each
peptide across all drugs and concentrations. The goal is to identify peptides
that are broadly drug-sensitive and to characterize the direction and strength
of their responses. We analyzed 2,435 peptides that passed the 80% coverage
filter, testing each one against every drug using linear regression on
log2-transformed concentration values. Significance was assessed at alpha =
0.05 after Benjamini-Hochberg correction for multiple testing.


Overall Response Landscape

The overwhelming finding is that most peptides are unaffected by drug
treatment. Out of 2,435 peptides, 2,404 (98.7%) were classified as
non-responsive, meaning they showed no statistically significant
dose-response relationship with any of the 46 drugs tested. Only 31 peptides
(1.3%) reached significance for at least one drug. Of these, 30 showed
decreasing abundance with increasing drug concentration, and just one showed
an increasing pattern. No peptide was significant for more than two drugs,
which means the dataset contains no broadly responsive peptides. This
indicates that the kinase-inhibitor panel does not cause widespread proteomic
disruption, and the peptides that do respond are affected by specific drugs
rather than by drug treatment in general.


Drug-Level Regression Results

Across all 112,010 peptide-by-drug regression tests, only 32 reached
statistical significance after multiple-testing correction. These 32
significant hits were concentrated in a small number of drugs. AT-9283
produced the most hits with 9 significant peptide responses, followed by
BMS-690514 with 7 and AV-412 with 6. Alvocidib accounted for 3 hits, AZD-5363
for 2, and five other drugs (ASP-3026, AZD-4547, Apitolisib, BI-2536, and
BMS-754807) each contributed a single significant peptide. The remaining 36
drugs in the panel produced no significant peptide-level dose-response
relationships at all.

The effect sizes among significant hits were small. The slopes of the
significant regressions ranged from -0.054 to +0.012, with a mean of -0.024
and a median of -0.021. Because the abundance data was min-max normalized on
a log2 scale to fall between 0 and 1, a slope of -0.02 represents
approximately a 2% decrease in the normalized scale per unit increase in
log2-concentration. While these effects are modest, the fact that they survive
stringent multiple-testing correction across over 100,000 tests suggests they
reflect real biological signals rather than noise. The strong negative bias
in the slopes is consistent with drug-induced target engagement, where
increasing inhibitor concentration reduces the binding or stability of
specific peptides.


Top Responsive Peptides

The peptide with the strongest response was GSQITQQSTNQSR, which showed a
significant decrease in abundance under Alvocidib treatment with the largest
absolute slope of 0.054. Two modified variants of this same peptide sequence
(carrying mass shifts of +102.034 and +43.006) showed identical behavior,
which is expected since they derive from the same underlying protein and
confirms that the signal is not an artifact of one particular peptidoform.

The second-strongest response came from LQPQEISPPPTANLDR, which was the only
peptide in the dataset to reach significance for two drugs (ASP-3026 and one
other). This makes it the closest candidate to a multi-drug-responsive
peptide, though two drugs is still far from a broadly responsive pattern.

Other notable hits include DLKVENILLHDR responding to AZD-5363, DVYEEDSYVKR
and GAQASSGSPALPR responding to BMS-690514, and HLFSTIPELINYHQHNSAGLISR
responding to AV-412. In several cases, modified variants of the same
underlying sequence appear as separate rows in the results, so the true
number of distinct biological signals among the top hits is smaller than the
raw count of 31 peptides suggests. Collapsing by unmodified sequence would
likely reduce this to roughly 15-20 unique responsive peptide groups.


Control-vs-Treatment Comparisons

As a complementary analysis, we compared each treatment concentration
directly against the DMSO control for every peptide-drug pair using the
Mann-Whitney U test. This analysis was largely uninformative due to the
experimental design. Each drug-concentration condition in this dataset has
only one technical replicate, which means that most pairwise comparisons
(94%, or 844,938 out of 896,080) could not be computed at all because the
test requires at least two observations per group. Among the approximately
51,000 comparisons that were computable, none reached significance after
Benjamini-Hochberg correction. The raw p-values for these tests were
generally large (median = 0.67), and after adjusting across nearly 900,000
tests, all adjusted p-values were 1.0. This result does not mean that there
are no real treatment effects. Rather, it reflects the fundamental limitation
that a non-parametric rank test cannot detect differences when comparing
single observations. This analysis would require biological replicates at
each concentration level to yield meaningful results.


Implications and Limitations

The regression-based dose-response approach proved to be the informative
method for this dataset. By pooling information across all 8 to 10
concentration levels per drug, the regression has enough data points to
detect monotonic trends even when each concentration is measured only once.
This is a key practical advantage over pairwise control-vs-treatment testing
in unreplicated experiments.

The finding that only 1.3% of peptides respond significantly to any drug,
and that all responses are drug-specific rather than broadly shared, has
biological implications. It suggests that the kinase inhibitors in this panel
act through specific molecular targets rather than causing general proteomic
stress. The concentration of hits in drugs like AT-9283, BMS-690514, and
AV-412 may reflect the potency of these particular compounds in the cell
line used, or it may indicate that their targets are more readily detected
at the peptide level in this assay.

A notable limitation is that the effect sizes are small in absolute terms.
Whether a 2-5% normalized scale change per log2-concentration unit is
biologically meaningful depends on the downstream context. These effects
could represent the tail of a larger biological response that is partially
masked by the normalization procedure, or they could reflect subtle but real
changes in target protein abundance or conformation. Future work could
validate these hits by examining protein-level aggregation, comparing against
known target annotations for each drug, and running the analysis on the
variant-specific (non-unmod) columns to see whether modification-specific
effects are detectable beyond the underlying peptide abundance changes.
